Ask five people what a Boston nanny costs and you'll get five answers. They're not all wrong; they're measuring different things.

## What the numbers say

| Source | What it measures | Boston rate |
|---|---|---|
| Nanny Lane | Average on nanny listings | about $22.61/hr |
| Nanny Lane | Part-time nanny listings | about $21.37/hr |
| Care.com | Average starting rate, child care providers (Apr 2026) | about $22.66/hr |
| Care.com | Average starting rate, newborn nannies (Jun 2026) | about $24.20/hr |
| DaycareCalc | Modeled cost to the family, taxes included | about $27/hr |
| The Nanny Haven (agency) | Typical pay for placed nannies | $30–35/hr |
| Boston Nanny Centre (agency) | Temporary caregivers | $30–35/hr |

## Why they disagree

- **Asking rates vs. what's actually paid.** Job sites show what nannies list, and many listings are students or part-timers. Career nannies placed by agencies earn more.
- **Before tax vs. all-in.** Some numbers are the nanny's hourly pay; others include your employer taxes.
- **Starting vs. typical.** "Starting rate" means the low end of the range, not the middle.

The honest answer: **a career nanny in Boston usually costs $25–35+ an hour** before employer taxes, and job-site averages understate it.

## What moves the rate

- **Experience and training:** years as a nanny, CPR, early-childhood education, newborn experience.
- **Number of kids:** more children usually means more per hour.
- **Hours:** full-time often earns a slightly lower hourly rate than a few short shifts; very long days usually earn more.
- **Extras:** driving, cooking, housekeeping, homework help, a second language.
- **Neighborhood:** Cambridge and the Newton suburbs tend to pay more (DaycareCalc puts them at about $28–36 an hour).
- **Nanny share:** two families split one nanny. Each family pays less per hour, and the nanny earns more in total.

## Don't forget the rest of the package

Most full-time nannies expect, and Massachusetts law requires some of:

- **Overtime** at 1.5× pay over 40 hours a week (live-in nannies included).
- **Paid time off and holidays**, by agreement.
- **Sick time** of up to 40 hours a year.
- **Guaranteed hours:** you pay the agreed hours even if you don't need them that week.
- **A raise:** many nannies expect one after the first year.

## A quick budget

At $28 an hour for 45 hours a week (40 regular plus 5 overtime), all year:

- Wages: 40 × $28 + 5 × $42 = **$1,330 a week**, about **$69,000 a year**
- Employer taxes: about 8–9% more ([see the taxes guide](/guides/massachusetts-nanny-taxes))
- Plus workers' comp insurance, and an agency fee if you use one ([see agency fees](/guides/nanny-agency-fees-boston))
