An agency does the part that keeps parents up at night: finding candidates, checking references and backgrounds, and standing behind the match. You pay for that, and the pricing is rarely on the front page. Here's how it works.

## What you're paying for

Most Boston placement agencies:

- **Screen candidates:** interviews, reference calls, background checks.
- **Match:** learn what your family needs and send a short list instead of a pile of profiles.
- **Guarantee the placement:** if it doesn't work out within a set window, they find a replacement.
- **Help with the paperwork:** many give you a template work agreement and point you to a payroll service.

What the fee **doesn't** cover: your nanny's wages and your employer taxes. You still pay both. (See [nanny taxes in Massachusetts](/guides/massachusetts-nanny-taxes).)

## How agencies charge

**A percentage of first-year pay, plus an upfront fee.** The placement fee scales with the salary, and most agencies also charge something up front to start the search:

| Agency | Upfront | Placement fee |
|---|---|---|
| Boston Nanny Centre | $500 engagement fee | 18% of first-year gross pay, $7,500 minimum |
| The Nanny Haven | $300 consultation | 15% of first-year expected salary, $4,500 minimum |

For a nanny earning $52,000 a year, that's about **$9,860** at Boston Nanny Centre and **$8,100** at The Nanny Haven.

**Success fee only.** Some recruiters charge nothing up front and bill only when you hire. Lighthouse Careers describes its Boston pricing this way and quotes the fee in a consultation.

## Older published fees, for context

Some Boston agencies used to publish flat placement fees. These are **older** and likely higher now, but they show the range:

| Agency (source) | Published fee |
|---|---|
| Boston Nanny Centre (Boston Voyager) | $3,500 full-time, $2,950 part-time; 6-month replacement; paid after a 30-day trial |
| The Washburn Agency (CBS Boston) | $250 application; $2,750 full-time, $2,200 part-time, $800 summer only |
| Beacon Hill Nannies (CBS Boston) | $395 application; $3,750 placement; 1-year replacement guarantee |

Compare that with today's percentage model: on a typical Boston salary, 15–18% lands well above these older flat fees.

## The guarantee matters as much as the fee

Ask exactly what happens if the match ends early:

- **How long is the guarantee?** The Nanny Haven lists a full year for full-time placements and three months for part-time. Older schedules above show six months to a year.
- **Replacement or refund?** Most offer a replacement search, not your money back.
- **What voids it?** Changing the job's hours, duties or pay often does.
- **When is the fee due?** At signing, at hire, or after a trial period.

## Temporary and backup care

Many agencies also place temporary nannies for travel, school breaks or gaps between nannies. Boston Nanny Centre's Harvard sheet lists **$75 per shift plus the caregiver's hourly rate**, with caregivers at about $30–35 an hour.

## Questions to ask every agency

1. What's the total fee, in writing, for my situation (hours, number of kids)?
2. Is it a percentage? Of what: hourly pay only, or including overtime and bonus?
3. When do I pay, and is anything refundable?
4. How long is the guarantee, and what voids it?
5. What screening do you do, specifically?
6. Do you help with the work agreement and payroll?
7. Do you offer discounts? Some do through employers, or seasonally.

## Agency or on your own?

An agency makes sense if you're short on time, want screening you trust, or need someone fast. Finding a nanny yourself through Facebook groups, neighbors or Care.com costs less but takes more of your time, and the vetting is on you. Plenty of families do both: they search on their own while an agency runs a parallel search.
